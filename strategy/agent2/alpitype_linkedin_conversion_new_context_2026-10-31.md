## 2. Crucial new observations from the founder

### A. The current LinkedIn audience is not the proposed buyer audience

The proposed target of business owners *without* internal software teams does not match the existing network, which is heavily technical and often employed at companies *with* development teams. There are two possible, **unvalidated** acquisition mechanisms:

1. Build a deliberate new network of relevant business owners and decision makers.
2. Achieve significant out-of-network distribution through technical engagement/reposts.

Neither mechanism has been demonstrated. Do **not** assume LinkedIn will naturally deliver posts to nontechnical buyers. Also do not assume companies with their own software teams cannot be customers; they may buy specialist capacity, integration, architecture, or external technical ownership.

### B. Engagement and interest do not translate into purchasing

The founder sees substantial AI interest and discussion in industrial/business circles but **low conversion to commissioned work**. His working explanation:

- Many buyers **cannot articulate their actual business/technical problem**.
- Many cannot connect a proposed AI solution to a credible, specific business benefit or operating model.
- Even after a solution is presented, they may not understand what they would receive, how it would operate, what risk it creates, or why they should authorize spending.
- Therefore, an acquisition strategy based on 'Tell us your automation problem' or 'Book a call with your use case' may be fundamentally misaligned with buyer readiness.

**Real anecdote:** A customer described a problem, received a proposed solution, did not understand it well enough, and now wants an additional **two-hour consultation** to develop confidence and a clearer vision of the proposed solution. The founder suspects the buyer needs to *see/understand/believe the future operating model*, not simply receive another technical proposal. This is one case, **not** proof of market-wide willingness to pay for standalone workshops.

### C. The competition problem is central

The founder explicitly asks:

> Why should a customer hire AlpiType for AI systems engineering instead of one of dozens of newly self-described AI experts who can prompt Claude, assemble n8n workflows, or deliver simple automations cheaply?

An answer based only on 'we know AI', 'we advise on AI', or 'we find use cases' is inadequate. The proposed differentiation must be *commercially legible and credible to a nontechnical buyer*, not just technically true.

Potential, **not yet validated**, AlpiType advantage: translating an ambiguous business objective into a production-worthy integrated software system, covering architecture, existing-system integration, data, exception handling, validation, operational ownership, and evolution. However, simple workflows may genuinely be better served by low-cost automation providers; avoid claiming AlpiType should win every AI project.

### D. The founder rejected invented offers and prices

Earlier assistant suggested a free orientation call, paid two-hour 'AI vision workshop' at EUR 290–490, and fixed-price technical scoping at EUR 1,500–3,000. **These prices were arbitrary and explicitly rejected.** Do not repeat, anchor on, or recommend prices without research and cost/effort/value evidence. Similarly, do not assume a workshop should be a standalone paid product just because a customer requested a two-hour explanation.

### E. Generic distribution/conversion features were rejected as non-implementable

Earlier suggestions such as 'identify potential clients and discussions for distribution' and 'assign conversion to each post' were called nonsense **as written**, because they lacked an actionable data source, implementation mechanism, attribution limitations, and a realistic workflow.

The existing content pipeline cannot reliably know who will see a post, identify which impressions came from purchasers, or attribute a later deal to a specific post. Do not disguise marketing wishes as engineering specifications.

## 5. What a credible buyer journey may need

**Not:** Viral post → buyer describes precise problem → generic AI workshop → automatic sale.

**Possible alternative to validate:** Buyer sees a *recognizable business scenario* → understands an achievable future workflow and its constraints → develops confidence in value and supplier → has a scoped decision conversation → authorizes discovery/prototype/implementation as appropriate.

Possible sales assets to test (not build all by default):

- A plain-language before/after operating scenario grounded in an actual industry workflow.
- A one-page *system vision* showing inputs, existing tools, AI decisions, human exception handling, outputs, accountability, and assumptions.
- Evidence of comparable delivery, transparent boundaries between standard SaaS/low-code/custom engineering, and who will own maintenance.
- An explicit 'when you do **not** need AlpiType' explanation to improve trust and avoid commoditized work.

Important: the founder's customer may need **decision confidence**, not more AI education. Diagnose which missing piece matters (economic value, visual clarity, technical risk, organizational risk, supplier trust, or budget) rather than asserting a single cause.
