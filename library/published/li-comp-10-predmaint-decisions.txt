84 percent of predictive maintenance pilots are still pilots a year later.

The model is rarely the reason. The reasons are four decisions that were never made.

1. Where does the analytics run?
Cloud bills you monthly for data and compute. On-premise bills you for the people who run the server. Edge bills you for every update to a machine in the field. For a proof of concept we start central. The production architecture is decided after we have seen the data, not before.

2. Which competencies do you buy, and which do you build?
The competence you do not have is a recurring cost either way. Decide early who carries it.

3. Which data do you actually have?
Failures are rare. Most machines log cycle counts and error codes, not motor currents and travel times. So the first useful result is condition monitoring: a baseline, trends a service technician recognises, interpretable thresholds. Prediction comes when the history exists.

4. Time to solution or time to production?
A working PoC takes weeks. Production means security review, write-back into the service workflow, rollout across machine variants. The gap between the two is where most pilots die, because it was never budgeted.

None of this requires rebuilding the machine. It requires looking at what the machine already measures and being honest about what is missing.

If you build machines and are weighing these decisions: 30 minutes with an engineer, no sales team.
